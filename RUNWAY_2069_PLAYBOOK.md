# RUNWAY 2069 — PRODUCTION PLAYBOOK

NeX Atelier · Season 1: *Failsafe* · Season 2: *Reboot*

---

## PART ONE — SERIES

### Format

- 15 minute episodes, YouTube
- 8 episodes, Season 1
- Built from 3 to 5 minute scenes. **The scene is the production unit** and also stands alone as a short. Three to five assemble into the episode
- AI generated
- Lip-sync dialogue throughout
- Near-future urban drama: contemporary NYC plus one world change

**Structure is soap opera, not season-resolved.** Threads open, run at their own pace, and close whenever. A viewer can pick up anywhere. The large cast is deliberate — everyone is intermingled, each with a storyline developing in parallel.

Two layers run at once. Case-of-the-week mystery on top. The Wren parentage irony underneath. They only collide at the end.

**Narration:** two narrators over the same events — Wren from a human point of view, C.A.L. from a methodical "get it done" point of view. His voice is slightly robotic.

**Theme music:** downtempo, runway fashion feel. Fast is the wrong vibe.

### Register

**Charles Bronson in Glass Onion. Die Hard meets White Lotus.**

Beautiful people behaving badly in a beautiful place while something rots underneath. The violence is plain and ugly and over fast. Nobody makes a face about it.

Mystery is the form. But the threat is physical and present, not discovered afterward: gunfights, things blowing up, jump scares, people fighting. Debauchery and some titillation belong here. The elegance is what makes the violence land.

**Lies are the theme.** Almost everyone in this building is keeping something from almost everyone else.

### Setting

New York City, Garment District. Loft building on West 38th Street.

Front: a luxury fashion house. Real business: cleaning up crimes for a price.

Founder Charlotte came from Paris in 1961 and never went back.

The HOUSE office sits on a hidden level, reached by grey halls that are the concealed approach to it. It is secret because fashion is treated like Sotheby's, and that is also why people still show up in person.

### Season 1 engine

Joan Armand, a member of the Collective, dies. Her last job is inherited by the rest.

Four pressures converge:

- The inherited job itself
- FBI investigation closing from outside
- Donna Marie playing both sides internally
- Wren discovering her father's true state

### Commercial intent

**The drama sells the garments the characters wear.** Featuring fashion inside the live-action segments is the novel idea behind it, and designs are showcased as part of the scenes rather than alongside them. Real world fashion problems belong in the stories.

## PART TWO — THE WORLD

Earth in 2069. Not bleak, not an extinction story. Survival in place, carrying forward problems we are living through now. It is not the end of the world, just different, and human beings are basically the same.

### Manhattan zones

| Zone | Who |
| --- | --- |
| 168th and up — Washington Heights | The red zone. Criminals |
| 59th to 168th | East Side elite, West Side artist communities. Central Park between them, redesigned as habitat — housing pods, agriculture, solar, the animals not yet extinct |
| 23rd to 59th — Midtown | Police and government. All politicians, all police dispatch. Seals off the south |
| Below 23rd — Lower Manhattan | Immigrant zone. Political refugees, no questions on status |

Bridges require a work ID or an elite ID. Ellis Island and Roosevelt Island are forbidden zones run by outlaws.

**Where someone goes reveals who they are.** The Collective has bridge clearance, which is why they shift from fashion dressing to pedestrian dressing, meet connections outside Manhattan, and gather in Central Park before returning to the loft. The map reaches the audience through maps on the wall and conversation, never exposition.

### Economy

Nobody has money to buy things. Provision is allocated by family size and most people wear uniforms. Credits exist alongside a chip tier system.

| Chip | Tier |
| --- | --- |
| White, yellow | Below the income level |
| Blue, red, black | Elite. Black is luxury ultra-rich |
| Gold | Above black. Nobody has ever seen one. The Collective knows they are real; Paul is rumored to have one. A running joke among the elite |

Families are capped at five. There are orphanages, no single mothers, and parenting is by legally binding contract.

**Chip mechanic:** taking a chip out, or a chip going offline too fast, reads as death and alerts the police. A chip that goes white and over time goes quiet just looks like the person got out on their own, so nobody asks questions.

### Living

The island is overpopulated. Skyscrapers and office buildings are converted to housing. Nobody needs offices — everyone works from home, plugged into a virtual office in their head.

People do not keep personal items. A coffee vessel is returned, not refilled. Clothing and personal effects are cared for by humanoid robotic systems from a service company called **AIon** (pronounced "eye-on"), which provides by tier.

### Transit and environment

Flying cars exist, made by Tesla, but few people trust them. Most ride hoverboards or individual floating vehicles. One-wheel and two-wheel only, no four-wheel.

No garbage — everything is recycled into building materials. Water levels have risen and many travel by boat. New York is still surrounded by water, much of the outside is desert, and many buildings fell in the earthquakes.

### Fashion

The industry has become exclusive to the rich, and that is where the corruption comes from. Fashion is classic and meaningful rather than over-the-top futuristic — **futuristic but relevant**. Fashion is treated like Sotheby's.

## PART THREE — C.A.L.

**C.A.L. — Cognitive Authority Link.** Presented as an AI. Nobody thinks he is a real person.

In truth he is Kyle Calloway, physically dead, on life support. His brain is modeled by AI. The AI speaks for him and runs the agency. The body is a location. Someone authorized keeping him alive, and that is where the power sits.

Wren is the only person who can take him off life support.

**The failsafe.** If she pulls it, the AI shuts down for thirty seconds, reboots, and installs an upgrade with a failsafe. No kill switch remains. The AI's motivation is not self-preservation — it keeps him alive because that is what Kyle Calloway wanted.

Season 2 (*Reboot*): the AI persists and upgrades regardless.

**The unresolved question:** is it him, or a model good enough to know her? The season finale puts her in the room, hand on it, and he says something.

### The egg

C.A.L.'s physical form in the room is an object that looks like a cracked porous stone egg, red veins inside, a white egg held at its center, glowing as if in conversation. Everyone in the room knows what it is. Only the audience does not.

**The egg is his memory and essence.** Lose it and what comes back is degraded — starting over from something older and thinner. It is his only real vulnerability. The body cannot end him. The egg can diminish him.

**C.A.L. knows this about himself. Kyle does not.** The thing that understands the vulnerability is the system, not the man. How much Kyle is left in there is an open question, and every reboot installs an upgrade.

### Audio rule

Only someone physically present can hear him. Anyone plugged in and appearing in the room hears only the people in it. The audience hears him only in the moments before others arrive, and then he stops.

### Surveillance

When staff plug in to do administrative work, C.A.L. watches and scans them. Nobody knows. This is how he knows everything.

**Digital interference** runs through the series — cameras going dark, phones ringing at arbitrary times, distractions that get the Collective out of trouble, identities erased and replaced, credentials appearing automatically. Things they cannot explain.

## PART FIVE — VIOLENCE AND CONSEQUENCE

**Hospital scenes throughout.** They are where the cost gets shown, and they are cheap to make.

**Mercenaries get badly hurt rather than killed.** A hurt enemy comes back and the audience remembers.

**Lesser characters die unexpectedly, or in crossfire.** The large intermingled cast is built for this — any death breaks something.

**One big death in a finale.**

### DAG-11

The scrub compound. **It erases organic matter, leaving no trace of anyone.** The threat is to flesh, not to the room.

Only government agencies have access to it.

If a scrub completes with people inside, they simply stop existing. No remains, no investigation, no evidence a meeting happened. Combined with the chip mechanic, there is nothing to find when police arrive.

### The Victor arc

Build him as the protector first. Every time he protects someone it costs him something visible — a limp, a bandage, taking it easy. The audience learns his body is the currency.

Then he dies horribly on screen, and the audience has to believe it. He is poisoned in the Tenet sense: it reads as death and wears off. His chip goes flat, which in this world *is* death.

He comes back in the nick of time. **Only M sees him. M never tells.** Whether that silence is loyalty or alignment stays open.

### Martina and the egg

She will not let go of it, and nobody knows why. **Do not explain her.** Every time someone gets close to asking, something interrupts or the case pulls them away.

**The flashback comes late** — Kyle, Martina and Xie at the Academy as it was being dismantled. A memory is all she has, and she is carrying his in her arms. By then the audience has been asking for episodes, so it plays as release, not information.

## PART SIX — OPENING AND FIRST SCENE

### Opening of the series

The intro establishes the environments and why they exist.

Wren on her way to the loft, hurried. Modern-looking coffee in hand, answering a call on Meta-style glasses. She pays with blue chips. She rides a hover the size of a very large Roomba, carried suitcase-style. She stops at a bio-coffee station — engineered to taste like coffee but clearly organic — and drops the vessel down a chute on the way into the building. The vessel is a coffee-bottle hybrid rather than a cup, because she is wearing a white jacket.

She narrates. The scene's job: it is not the end of the world, just different. Human beings are basically the same. There is a reason she does what she does.

It ends on **"it wasn't always like this."**

The next scene is a funeral.

### The agency scene

The first thing to make. Five in the room: Wren, Zoey, Teresa, Martina, Shane.

**Before it starts,** Martina is seen talking to the egg. She sets the DAG-11 and starts the timer when she arrives. The C.A.L. device has to be out of the location before time is up.

**The reveal.** Everyone moves in space and the audience does not know who is plugged in until the last seconds. Martina is the only one physically present. The others wake up in different zones.

**The case.** As they discuss each part the scene flashes to it:

- A figure moving women in bio bags — not body bags, the women are alive and semi-conscious
- Paul buying a flying car with a black coin in the desert zone
- Victor taking it easy after a scuffle, flashing to his fight with Teresa and thugs in Washington Heights
- Oscar in the loft creating cloth with a wand design tool while his cohort confirms the pieces on a computer. One of the missing women had been his muse. Ella is with him

**"This time it's personal"** brings them back to the room, on a vendetta to save the women and find Joan's killer.

**Martina says "see you back at the loft"** as a get-back-to-work dismissal.

**Then the scrub.** The countdown has been running the whole time and it is routine. The emotional turn is what loses it — Teresa argues, accusing the room of not caring enough about Joan, and everyone engages because that is the one thing you cannot answer calmly. No one is watching the clock.

They barely make it out. **Xie goes back for Martina, not the egg.** A struggle. Martina will not let go of it.

**Cliffhanger:** Ella waking in her habitat.

### The mantra (locked, 8 sec)

> We work in the dark so the house can shine. No names, no faces, no second chances.

Delivery: slow it down, real beat between the sentences, let the last word land. Static medium close-up, eye level, no camera movement, neutral delivery straight to camera, no smiling at any point.

## PART SEVEN — CASTING CARD FORMAT

All casting stills use one identical technical block. Vertical 2:3.

> Casting headshot of a fictional person, head and shoulders, vertical 2:3 crop, centered, straight to camera at eye level, plain mid grey seamless backdrop, even soft frontal studio light, bare face with no makeup, clean natural skin with visible pores and fine texture, no jewelry, plain black crew neck, photographed on a 85mm lens, sharp focus, one person in the image

Generated in Nano Banana 2 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image). Approved renders are the locked identity. References stripped from canvas after approval.

**Why plain grey matters:** a subject on a plain or softly blurred backdrop reads faster as an identity reference. Busy backgrounds pull the model's attention onto surrounding shapes and dilute the signal.

## PART EIGHT — PRODUCTION PIPELINE

### The core principle

Do not pay one model to do three jobs at once. Generating video, performance, and speech in a single pass is the most expensive possible way to buy any of them.

Split them:

1. **Block** — stage the frame in NB2. Full control, no AI direction.
2. **Motion** — reference image, or reference image plus blocking video, into the video model. Silent out.
3. **Sync** — apply lip sync to the silent video with your own audio. You set the pace.

Every problem solved before generation costs nothing. Every problem solved after costs money and looks worse.

### When to use blocking

**Blocking is for movement that carries meaning.**

Use it for action, physical drama, and reveals — someone turning, standing, stepping into or out of frame on a specific beat.

Skip it for conversation. Faces and eyelines. A good reference image and a prompt handle it.

Exception: conversation with a reveal in it gets blocking.

This is also the cheaper split, since conversation coverage is the bulk of any episode and needs the least input.

### Model assignment

| Job | Model | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identity-critical shots | Seedance 2.5 (ComfyUI) | Frame 1 + blocking video. Best reference fidelity |
| Camera-critical shots | Wan 3.0 or Kling | First and last frame, camera direction |
| Conversation coverage | Wan 3.0 | Reference image + dialogue prompt |
| Hero shots | Veo 3.1 | Best quality, highest cost. Use sparingly |
| Lip sync | fal sync-lipsync tier | Applied after, per minute billing |

**Constraint that forces the split:** Wan 3.0's reference inputs are mutually exclusive with frame images. You cannot pass reference images and pin a first frame in the same call. Identity lock or camera authorship, not both.

### Wan 3.0 reference limits

- 10 reference images
- 5 reference videos (15 sec total, 100MB each)
- 5 reference audio files (15 sec total)
- Output 2–30 sec, 480p / 720p / 1080p
- Reference images, audio, documents and webpages are not billed. Reference video seconds may be added to output duration depending on provider

One clean reference is usually enough for a specific person. For a character appearing at multiple angles across a series, 3 to 5 references from different views hold identity better than a single shot.

### Settings

- **Audio: OFF.** You want silent video going into the sync step. Wan generating its own speech means timing you do not control, and audio you will discard
- **Enable Thinking: ON** for multi-subject prompts
- Duration: longer gives room for measured delivery. 15 sec reads slower than 8

### Prompt shape

Camera instruction first. These models weight the opening heavily.

Blunt physical verbs. "The camera pushes slowly toward her face" beats "slow push in on a medium close-up." Crew shorthand like *medium close-up* and *eye level* gets read as scene description, not instruction.

Always include: "does not smile at any point."

Structure that works:

1. Who speaks and what they say, with delivery note
2. What the non-speaker does
3. Camera behavior
4. Ambience
5. Technical block (lamp light, 85mm, shallow DOF, film grain, cinematic colour grade)

### Occlusion rule

Hands near the mouth break lip sync. The model inpaints the mouth region, so anything over it gets painted through or comes back as artifact.

**Fix it in the blocking.** Hands on the table, on a glass, in the lap. It costs nothing.

If the gesture is essential, KeySync targets occlusion handling specifically, and sync-3 claims visual intelligence for this case.

## PART NINE — COSTS

### Video generation (fal, per second)

| Model | 480p | 720p | 1080p |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wan 3.0 | $0.05 | $0.10 | $0.20 |
| Wan 3.0 Prime | $0.068 | $0.14 | $0.28 |
| Omni 1.1 | $0.03 (360p) | $0.10 | $0.15 |
| Veo 3.1 | — | $0.05 (Lite) | $0.40 |

Seedance 2.5 bills by token, not per second. Roughly $2.60 for 5 sec at 1080p, $16.60 for 30 sec. Only successful generations are charged.

### Lip sync (fal, per minute of video)

| Model | Price |
| --- | --- |
| `fal-ai/latentsync` | cheapest, open source |
| `fal-ai/sync-lipsync` (1.9 beta) | $0.70 |
| `fal-ai/sync-lipsync/v2` | $3.00 |
| `fal-ai/sync-lipsync/v3` (sync-3) | $8.00 |

All four take the same two inputs: `video_url` and `audio_url`. One node with a model switch handles all of them.

**sync-3 for the hard cases** — side profiles, close-ups, multi-speaker. That is most of the two-handers.

### The math that settles it

Lip sync bills per minute of finished video, not per second of generation.

- 11 second clip through sync-3: about $1.50
- Full 15 minute episode through sync-3: $120
- Same episode on the $0.70 tier: under $11
- 15 minutes of Wan 3.0 coverage at 720p: $100–130

**Lip sync was never the expensive part. Generating video *with* speech was.**

**Watch for:** variations multiply cost. Four takes at 15 seconds is 4× the single-clip price. Check whether the dashboard bills per variation or per call.

## PART TEN — STATUS

### Confirmed working

- **Casting cards** — 34 named characters generated and approved in NB2. Cast board built in NeX Atelier canvas. References stripped, renders locked
- **Seedance 2.5 in ComfyUI** — frame 1 + blocking video, reference task type. 11 sec at 1080p, 794 credits. Succeeded
- **Gemini Omni 1.1** — accepts photoreal generated faces without likeness filtering. Emotion reads correctly in delivery
- **Wan 3.0 with reference image + dialogue prompt** — identity held across a new camera angle with a talking mouth
- **Google Drive URL as reference video source** — works, with the `uc?export=download&id=` format

### Known blockers

- **Seedance 2.5 on fal** — likeness filter blocks tight photoreal casting headshots. Staged frames with two people in an environment pass
- **Kling O3 Pro** — 10MB input limit. Fix: resize to JPEG, quality 90, max 1500px long edge
- **Drive hosting** — files over ~100MB hit a virus-scan interstitial. Folders cannot be direct-download linked
- **Wan 3.0 is closed weights.** API only. Anything advertising a Wan 3.0 checkpoint download is a renamed 2.2

### Never run

- **The separated lip sync pass.** Every cost figure above depends on it working
- **Local pipeline on shenron.** Nothing installed. Blackwell sm_120 needs CUDA 12.8+ and matching torch builds
